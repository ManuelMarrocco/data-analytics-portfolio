Charts created and insights written:

Main contributors to global emissions
The analysis shows that CO2 emissions are highly concentrated among a limited number of countries. During the period 1987–2011, the United States and China are the main contributors, with approximately 13.3 and 10.9 billion tons of CO2 respectively. The other countries show significantly lower cumulative emissions: India and Japan produce slightly less than 3 billion tons, followed by the United Kingdom, Canada, and Italy. This distribution highlights a strong concentration of global emissions within a small group of major economies.

Evolution of emissions over the last 25 years
The trend analysis reveals different emission patterns among the main emitting countries. China, India, South Korea, South Africa, and Mexico show a clear growth trajectory during the analyzed period. In contrast, Canada, Italy, and the United States show moderate growth until the early 2000s, followed by a slight decrease. Japan maintains relatively stable emission levels throughout the entire period, while the United Kingdom shows a gradual reduction in emissions toward the end of the analyzed time interval.

Countries with the fastest emission growth
Among the analyzed countries, China emerges as the country with the fastest emission growth, with an increase significantly higher than any other country in the dataset. The growth rate observed in China is approximately four times higher than that of India, the second country in terms of emission growth, reflecting the rapid industrial and economic expansion that occurred during the analyzed period.

Countries with relatively stable emissions
A group of countries shows relatively stable emission levels over time, with minimal long-term variations. Many of these countries are smaller economies or island states, where emission levels remain relatively constant throughout the analyzed period.

Countries reducing emissions
The analysis also identifies countries where emissions decreased over time. In particular, the United Kingdom, together with countries such as Poland and Romania, shows a clear decreasing trend in annual CO2 emissions during the analyzed period. This indicates that while some economies continue to increase their emissions, others are already going through a gradual reduction phase.
